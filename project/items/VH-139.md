# VH-139 — A file that ends early is said before Create and in the record

## Intent

V-01 of the 20 October audit, rank 1. A recording whose data stops before its index says — an interrupted copy, a crashed recorder — is read as whole, checked as "Ready to go", encoded, and recorded as "the whole video (30 seconds)", while the file made carries 21 s of sound under 30 s of picture and a held frame. Nothing warns. The rules call silent data loss the worst outcome; this item makes the page say what each track really holds, before Create where it can be known then and in the result where it can only be known then.

## Acceptance criteria

- Pre-flight's analysis pass compares the sound's decoded extent with the kept range; when it falls short by more than a configured margin (a number in `src/config/`), a sound note says so before Create, with the time the sound stops at ("The file ends early: its sound stops at 20 s of 30."), in all three languages, announced with the verdict as the other notes are.
- When the encode decodes fewer frames than the kept range declares, the result says so beside the record, and the record gives the length made, never the length promised; the discard question carries the same.
- A source with no sound, and a source whose sound and picture legitimately differ in length by less than the margin, raise no note; a trimmed source is measured against the part kept.
- The 3-second probe is not asked to certify the tail; the output check is not asked to measure completeness beyond the encode's own count.
- A fixture cut short at 70% of its bytes (30 s declared, about 21 s decodable, as `reviews/2026-10-20/README.md` records) shows the note before Create and the length made in the record; tests by category; `npm run check` green with no new skip; checked in Chrome.
- The spec's §7.2 and §5.4 rows for the new note are a wish line for the owner (docs/ is protected).
