# VH-192 — Watch the finished video before saving it

## Intent

After a video is made, there is no way to watch it before saving; the line after saving says "Open the saved file to check it". The owner asked for this work in PLAN-8, as the owner's call on top of the simplicity rule (VH-182.6). The smallest form, recommended on the wording sheet (question Q3), is the browser's own player, the same native `<video controls>` step 2 already uses, inside the finished video's card. It plays the new file from the device's working storage, with nothing playing until pressed. Step 2's player cannot be reused: it shows the original as the trim handles move, and the step says so. This is a new element, though not a new kind of control, and the sheet says so plainly. Spec §9.1 step 6 and §9.3 change by corrections S20 and S21.

## Acceptance criteria

- The owner's Q3 choice, option A, is quoted in this item's close.
- Under option A, the finished card holds one native player with the browser's own controls, named by the card's heading, preloading metadata only, never autoplaying, and with no new words (sheet B10).
- It plays the file in place from the working storage (OPFS) and never reads the whole file into memory (the rules' streaming line); its object URL is revoked when the result is discarded, replaced or the tab is closed, and cancel and discard still leave no orphaned data.
- A failed playback leaves the result, the save and the record unchanged; the existing "Previous video" card behaves as the finished one does, or the close says why not.
- The finished video's own additions, the title or submission card and the closing graphics, are assessed for 1.2.x and 2.3.x in the close: silent, no flashing, and their words chosen on this page; spec §9.3's exception reads as S21 approves, which covers them apart from the user's recording.
- No other control is added; focus after a job's end still goes where VH-178's rule sends it.
- Spec §9.1 step 6 and §9.3 read as corrections S20 and S21 approve, applied in this item's commit, within the spec's 8,100 words.
- Checked in Chrome at 1280, 375 and 320 px, in light, dark and forced colours, and in three languages; `npm run check` green with no test deleted or skipped; the digest rule applied named at close (rule 8); Codex astra checks the candidate diff before commit, and the commit carries its `Checked:` line.
