**Revise the audit before implementing it wholesale.** Its strongest proposals remove real duplication and misleading status. Its weakest substitute visual uniformity for task hierarchy, accept “Keep” without examining recovery paths, and claim broader screenshot coverage than the evidence provides.

This is a read-only review against **`339cf57`**. I changed no files and ran no app or tests. Product files changed concurrently during review; those later edits are outside this critique. Source citations below refer to the stated baseline.

The evidence needs correction first:

- **Three phone captures miss their named state.** `06-reading-phone.png` shows **checking**, `15-cancelling-phone.png` shows **cancelled**, and `20-saving-phone.png` shows **saved**. Their JSON entries agree. These are not evidence of the intermediate states described in rows 06, 15 and 20.
- **The JSON is not exclusively visible text.** It includes visually hidden legends, spoken-only verdicts and the hidden progress label. For example, `08-ready` contains the full verdict twice, although the screenshot displays it once. The hidden announcement is intentional: `src/main.ts:426`, `src/main.ts:443`; hidden progress naming is at `index.html:340`. Do not count these as visible duplication or delete them.
- **Coverage is uneven.** Phone text has 29 entries; desktop text has two; reflow, dark and forced-colour text stop at state 16. Later PNGs cover desktop and phone, not the full matrix. The opening “screenshots of every state” claim needs qualification.
- **Capture provenance needs explaining.** The screenshots display build identity `e979810`, while the audit names `339cf57`. A long-running development server could explain that, but the evidence does not establish the explanation.
- **Row 13 is actually the trimmed-ready capture**, with a mobile discouragement verdict. Row 08 on phone is also discouraged. The audit should distinguish platform, selection and verdict instead of treating them as interchangeable state names.

These limitations do not invalidate source-confirmed defects. They invalidate claims that those transitions have already been visually examined.

For the **six “readings of the brief”**, I would make these decisions:

1. **“Said once” — retain the interpretation, correct its attribution.** Persistent privacy and original-safety reassurance in failures are explicit requirements. Nonblocking sound warnings are also required in meaning; their exact two-sentence reassurance is implementation wording, not untouchable specification text. Necessary explanation near an attempted action can repeat context. Equally, do not classify hidden announcements as visible repeats.

2. **“One thing to do” — retain.** Waiting is a legitimate current task, Cancel remains available, and using the saved file may happen outside the app. Add the missing qualification: a failure’s next action depends on its cause. “Try again” is not the universal continuation.

3. **Long labels — overturn the blanket exemption.** “Quoted elsewhere” is not a design reason to retain a label. “Over generated freeze frame” is established terminology; “Set start here” already meets the three-word limit; “Use the whole video” is explicitly named in spec §9.1. These have stronger reasons to stay. “Copy message and details” should be judged for clarity and wrapping; changing it means updating references, not reopening product policy. My earlier suggestion to shorten the reset label was too casual about the specification’s wording.

4. **Singleton title size — retain.** One title can legitimately occupy one level of the tokenised type scale. Adding another use merely to satisfy a count would make hierarchy worse.

5. **In-flow toggletip — overturn the claimed sufficiency, not necessarily the layout choice.** Non-obscuration is a sound benefit. It does not settle dismissal, page expansion, spacing or its visual resemblance to a warning. Record the deliberate Carbon adaptation and its full interaction contract.

6. **Secondary discouragement acknowledgement — retain as a contextual exception.** The warning can lead while continuing remains available. A filled button does not inherently endorse danger, so the audit’s rationale is overstated; the stronger reason is a clear, deliberate choice without making risky continuation dominant.

Several omissions from the earlier audit still require a decision. They should not disappear merely because the merged table is shorter:

- **Startup pending and startup blocked**, including what explains the disabled picker locally (`src/main.ts:810`, `src/ui/failure-text.ts:123`).
- **Check failed → retry → stopped**, and storage-block recovery—not just their isolated endpoints.
- **Preview unavailable**, whose asynchronous failure reveals text without an explicit announcement at that handler (`src/main.ts:1376`).
- **Changed closing and output-warning results**, including after another file is selected.
- **Save stopped, picker cancelled, original refused and save failed**. In particular, “Nothing was kept where you were saving it” overclaims: cleanup may leave an empty destination file when removal is unavailable or fails (`src/main.ts:2170`, `src/media/save.ts:160`).
- **Open Video properties and System check**, including standalone lowercase values and long labels (`src/main.ts:553`). Capitalising only the three promises does not finish the stated typography pass.
- **The blue swatch on a blue segment**, explicitly handed to VH-124 by VH-112 (`pm_skills/project/decision-log.md:68`; `src/styles/app.css:946`).
- **Internal step anatomy.** Trim’s result precedes its reset action (`index.html:168`); File size / quality needs no invented result sentence because its radio already communicates the choice. Consistent rhythm does not require identical content.
- **Whole-page competition.** “Beyond the frame” excludes the persistent introduction and prominent footer from the very whole-page judgement the ticket requests. Their presence is required; their relative emphasis still needs designing.

**The three changes I would make before anything else are:**

1. **Fix the entry hierarchy:** one properly labelled native chooser, primary only when choosing leads; remove the empty-state echo and introductory lead; finish the three promises.
2. **Make status transitions truthful:** clear stale blocks and failures when rechecking starts; protect cancellation and failure presentation; end saving presentation at confirmed delivery while retaining safe cleanup.
3. **Make the finished result the clear destination:** immutable identity, actual outcome and warnings together, one lifetime explanation, primary Save, quieter Create, and a separate safe inline discard confirmation.

**The one change I would refuse is turning the result and Discard/Keep confirmation into the same generic inline-notification component.** Consistent styling is useful; erasing their different purposes would weaken the page’s most consequential decisions.
