## Verdict

The page holds together as one design. Each state has at most one filled
action, and it is the right one. Every text element in 691 records sits
on one of seven drawn type roles, one look each. Every running-text block
stops at 448 px but one. The four keyboard routes passed 238 of 238 steps
that could be read. Contrast passed in 44,919 text rows and reflow in all
691 records, in three languages, seven views and eight modes. The six
fixes hold or are partial; none regressed.

What does not hold is narrow and mostly about focus:

- The file button's focus ring is clipped to a sliver in every state that
  shows it (V-40).
- At a larger default font the trim handles lose their ring (V-41).
- Under a light contrast theme the language switcher's ring is lost where
  it meets the next segment (V-42).
- The file button's own words are not in its accessible name (V-43).
- The first action is still below the first screen on the Chinese and
  Malay pages, and at 320 px in all three (V-18, incomplete).
- The feedback message still runs past the measure (V-33, incomplete).
- Two rank 3 points, hover under forced colours (V-44) and the closing's
  two names (V-45).

There are 8 draft findings, none at rank 1, 6 at rank 2 (two of them
incomplete fixes under their old IDs) and 2 at rank 3. None needs a new element. One,
V-43, reopens part of a recorded decision and is the owner's.

**The reading stated.** 1.4.8 sets one line length in two scripts, 80
characters, or 40 where the text is CJK. A CJK glyph therefore stands for
two characters. A mixed line is read by that weight, each CJK glyph two
and each Latin character one, against 80. On it the worst line is 66 of
80 (43 characters, 23 CJK), and every line passes. Read strictly, every
character against 40, all 86 records fail; that reading counts a narrow
Latin letter as a full-width glyph, which the criterion's own ratio does
not. Every one of these lines is also at most 448 px wide, 28 CJK glyph
widths. The reading is the same arithmetic as VH-167's half-unit count,
reached here from the criterion's ratio rather than assumed; Codex
questioned the half-unit count in its blind review, so the reading is for
VH-174's critique to test. WCAG says nothing about mixed lines, and that
part stays unresolved.

### V-43 The file button's own words are not in its accessible name — rank 2, the owner's

**Saw.** The chooser's button shows the browser's words, "Choose File" in
an English Chrome. Its accessible name is the step heading, "1. Choose a
video", with the value "No file chosen" (`ax`, `S-03__en-GB__w1280__light`:

Malay page with an English browser the two never meet. VH-168 chose the

browser's words as a limitation; it did not weigh Label in Name.

**Standard.** WCAG 2.5.3 Label in Name (A); UI-STANDARDS, Content and
form ("Visible label text must match the accessible name").

**Direction.** For the owner, since it reopens part of VH-168's decision,
not because of the simplicity rule; no option adds an element:

- Recommended. Test in Chrome a name that begins with the button's own
  words and then gives the step, for example by listing the input itself
  before the heading in its `aria-labelledby`. If Chrome computes it, the
  name carries both.
- Otherwise, extend VH-168's recorded limitation to 2.5.3, with the
  speech check (task G) as its evidence.

**Source.** `index.html:123`–`130` (`aria-labelledby="choose-heading"`);
`project/decisions.md`, VH-168. **Evidence:** the `ax` summary in every
