# VH-93 — A feedback button

> **Status:** Band 4 · [sign-off]. Working detail only; the decision
> rationale goes to `decision-log.md` when the item ships.

## Intent

Maintainer request, 2026-09-30: a feedback button that offers a text box and
a send button, and mails `joe.bell@nottingham.ac.uk` with the message and the
usage logs.

## Done when

See the backlog line.

## Evidence / context

**There is no server.** The app is static files, hosted as a flat Xerte
package inside a same-origin frame (checked 2026-09-30: no `sandbox`, no
`allow`). A browser cannot send email; the user's mail app can, or an
endpoint can.

**"No media egress, ever"** forbids any fetch, upload, beacon or analytics
call carrying media, filenames, or media *characteristics*. The diagnostics
bundle keeps characteristics on purpose — `core/redact.ts` allows dimensions,
duration, frame rate and codec, and strips filenames, paths, titles and
caption text — and it is dev-only today. This is its first production
surface, which `UI-STANDARDS.md` allows only with a redaction review.

**The bundle is too big to post whole.** Up to 500 log records from two
threads. Some mail clients cut a `mailto:` body at about 2,000 characters.

## Approach

### Option A — `mailto:` (recommended)

Send opens the user's own mail app: the recipient, a subject carrying the
build identity, the message, and a compact redacted summary in the body. The
user reads it and presses send.

- The app makes no request. The invariant, the egress watch and criterion 9
  are untouched, and the promise stays checkable in the network panel.
- The user sees every word and sends it as themselves, so a reply reaches
  them with no address field to fill in.
- The summary: version and build id, browser, stage reached, the three
  choices, the verdict and its reason codes, the source's shape, the last
  error, the last few log lines. The full bundle goes to the clipboard, with
  a line saying to paste it in if asked.
- The app cannot know the mail was sent. The dialog says "Your email app
  should have opened", never "Sent".
- A device with no mail app gets nothing, so the dialog also shows the
  address and a "Copy message and details" button.

### Option B — a relay endpoint

Send posts the message and bundle to something that mails it.

- One click, no mail app needed, and the app knows whether it arrived.
- It is the app's first outbound request with a body. The invariant has to
  be reworded, criterion 9 has to allow exactly one URL, and "verifiable by
  inspecting network activity" then means reading a payload, not seeing none.
- The URL ships in a public bundle from a public repository. If it carries a
  signature it is a committed credential, against the security baseline; if
  it does not, anyone can post to it.
- Someone has to own the endpoint, rate-limit it and keep it alive.

## Constraints

- **The leave warning.** `warnBeforeLeaving` is armed while a job runs or a
  result is unsaved, and a `mailto:` navigation can fire `beforeunload`. The
  link must not be a same-frame navigation that raises "Leave site?" over a
  running encode. Verify in every engine, mid-job.
- A feedback redaction profile, reviewed for a reader who is not the
  maintainer's own machine. Over-redaction is the safe failure direction.
- Native `<dialog>`: focus trapped, Escape closes, focus returns to the
  button. An empty message is refused beside the field.
- The button sits on a permanent surface — VH-92's footer band — and is
  offered again in the errors panel, where feedback is most useful.
- It stays usable during a job. A hung job is the thing most worth
  reporting.
- Nothing is ever sent without an explicit press. No automatic reporting.

## Open questions

- A or B. Default A.
- Whether the video's shape is included by default, or behind a visible
  "Include details about my video" checkbox. Default: included, and shown in
  a read-only "What will be sent" disclosure — under A the user is the
  sender and can delete any line.
- The address is public either way, in the bundle and in the repository. A
  role address would survive a change of maintainer.
