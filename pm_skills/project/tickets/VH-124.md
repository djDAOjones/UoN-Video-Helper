# VH-124 — The page, made excellent

> **Status:** Band 6, [sign-off]. Working detail for the agent that takes it;
> the decision rationale goes to `decision-log.md` when it ships. Maintainer
> request, 2026-10-01: "an excellently accessible and usable UX, including
> text usage, design elements, layout — bulletproof, low friction, and simply
> excellent."

## Why this item exists

Band 6 fixed what the 2026-10-01 review found wrong, one finding at a time.
Nobody has yet designed the page as a whole for the person who uses it: a
member of staff, in a hurry, who has never seen it, with a recording to
publish before a class. The maintainer's test of the live page on
2026-10-01 found step 1 saying "choose a video" four ways and the opening
bullets in lower case, neither of which any finding named. This item is the
whole-page pass those findings were too narrow to be, and it is judged as a
person judges a page, not as a checklist passes one.

## The brief — paste this to the agent that takes the item

You are designing the interface of UoN Video Helper, a single-page tool that
takes one recording and gives back a branded, levelled MP4. Read
`docs/01-specification.md` §9 (the workflow and principles), `UI-STANDARDS.md`
(Carbon productive, WCAG 2.2 AAA, the brand rules), and the 2026-10-01 UX
review's "What held up" and "What only a person can judge" before you touch
anything. Then make the page excellent, by these standards:

**One thing to do at every moment.** At any point the page shows the user
one obvious next action and nothing that competes with it. Before a file is
chosen, the page is the file control and the three-line promise — nothing
else needs to be read. After a file is read, the eye lands on the verdict and
Create. During a job, on the progress and Cancel. After, on Save. Everything
else is there, but quieter: secondary actions as secondary buttons, facts
behind a disclosure, help as a toggletip. Nothing on screen asks for
attention it has not earned.

**Every sentence earns its place, and is said once.** Walk every state —
empty, reading, read, blocked, checking, ready, warned, discouraged, running,
cancelled, failed, finished, saving, saved, previous video, feedback — and
list every visible string in it. Cut any line that repeats what another line
in view already says (step 1 today: a heading "Choose a video", a label
"Video file", a hint "Or drop a video file here." and a status "Choose a
video to begin." — one of these is the instruction, the rest are echoes).
Cut any line a first-time user does not need to act. Keep a line only if
removing it would leave someone not knowing what happened or what to do.
The gate's readability check (`test/readability.test.ts`) still holds every
string that survives.

**Capitalisation and typography that read as finished.** Sentence case
throughout, as Carbon and `UI-STANDARDS.md` prescribe — and that means a
capital letter at the start of every line a person reads as a line,
including each list item: "Adds approved branding.", "Ensures consistent
audio levels.", "Outputs an optimised file type and size." The three bullets
are the maintainer's words (VH-85) and keep their words; they gain their
capitals and full stops, and `test/screen-text.test.ts` is updated to pin the
new form. Headings in sentence case; labels one to three words; no colons
after labels; no trailing punctuation on labels or headings; full stops on
sentences; one typeface scale with no size that appears once. The measure of
body text at about 70 characters (VH-112's token) and line height from the
Carbon scale.

**Layout that carries the sequence.** The five steps read as one path down
the page, with the same anatomy each — number, heading, the controls, the
step's own result line — and consistent spacing between them from the Carbon
spacing scale, no ad hoc values. Steps that are not yet available are not on
the page (they appear once a video is read, as now). Within a step, controls
sit in reading order with their helper text directly beneath, errors beside
the field they concern, and the step's result line last. At phone width the
same order holds in one column with nothing clipped and no horizontal
scroll; at desktop width the page does not stretch beyond a comfortable
reading width. Nothing moves when something loads: reserve the space, or
reveal below the fold.

**Controls that look like what they are.** VH-112's rules, applied
everywhere: one primary action per state; secondary actions visibly
secondary; disabled controls visibly disabled with the AAA pair kept; a
locked step says why it is locked; every control at or above 44 × 44 CSS px;
focus rings visible on every control in light, dark and forced colours;
swatches that show their colour in every state; nothing that reads as a
checkbox unless it is one. Native controls before custom ones.

**Status that is honest, in time.** The page says what is happening while it
happens, and what it said is withdrawn when it stops being true (VH-108's
rule). Every asynchronous wait shows that it is waiting and can be stopped
(VH-110). A failure says what happened, that the original is safe, and what
to do, once each.

**Accessible as a matter of course.** WCAG 2.2 AAA as `UI-STANDARDS.md` and
spec §9.3 set it: 7:1 text contrast in every colour context, 3:1 on
components and focus indicators, no colour-only meaning, full keyboard
operation in reading order with no trap, visible labels matching accessible
names for speech input, live regions for what the user did not cause and
nothing else (VH-109's rule), focus handed on by every control that removes
itself (VH-111), reflow at 320 px and 200% enlargement, text spacing
overrides survive, reduced motion honoured, forced colours drawn. The
exceptions spec §9.3 records are the only ones.

**Plain throughout.** No unit, codec or implementation word on the main
path; one name for each concept (levelling, closing, the two outputs); every
kept term and abbreviation with its meaning beside it; the reading level the
gate measures.

**Design within the system, never around it.** IBM Carbon's productive
patterns, implemented in the project's own code; the UoN brand tokens for
colour and type; the Carbon tokens for shape, spacing and status colour. No
new colour, size or spacing value outside the two token files. If a Carbon
pattern exists for a thing, use it; if you believe none fits, say so in the
decision log and why. The logo is drawn as the University draws it, and
nothing else is brand decoration.

**Method.** Before changing anything, write a one-page audit: for each state,
what is on screen, what a first-time user is meant to do, what competes with
it, what repeats, and what you will change. Put it in `reviews/<date>/`. Make
the changes. Then walk the result, keyboard-only and at phone width, in
headless Chrome, and record a screenshot of every state beside the audit, so
the maintainer can judge the page as a page. Pure text decisions live in
`src/ui/*.ts` with tests; markup in `index.html`; styles in `src/styles/`.
`npm run check` stays green throughout; `test/screen-text.test.ts` and the
readability check are updated for every pinned string that changes, never
weakened.

**Out of scope.** No new features, controls or settings; no folding of
finished steps (VH-97 stands); no change to the five-step order or to the
names the maintainer fixed (Cut, Fade, Slide, None; Larger / better, Smaller /
reduced; the privacy sentence) beyond their capitals and punctuation;
nothing that reads as marketing rather than a productive tool.

## Done when

See the backlog line. In short: the audit exists with before-and-after
screenshots of every state; every state has one clear next action; no
visible line repeats another in view; every list item and sentence is
capitalised and punctuated; layout, spacing and type come from the tokens;
every control meets VH-112's rules; `check` is green with the pins updated;
and the maintainer has walked the page and signed it off — the [sign-off]
flag is theirs.
