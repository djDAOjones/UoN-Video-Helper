# VH-31 — The size estimate is ~3.6x too high

Measured 2026-08-25 from a real run on the deployed site.

## What happened

`AMCS3059 - Module in a Minute`, 852 × 480, 2 m 10 s, 7.1 MB in. The app said
**"Estimated size 27.7 MB"**, then produced **7.5 MB**.

Reproduced exactly: `outputShapeFor` gives 1.47 Mbps video plus 192 kbps audio
at that shape, and `projectedOutputBytes` multiplies by duration — 27.1 MB.

## Why

The projection assumes the encoder spends its whole bitrate budget. It does
not. H.264 rate control is variable, and slide-and-talking-head content at
852 × 480 is extremely compressible, so it undershoots the target by a wide
margin. The estimate is therefore an upper bound presented as a prediction.

The error is not small or occasional: 3.6x on the first real file anyone ran.

## A second, smaller contributor (found 2026-08-25 in backlog review)

`outputShapeFor` always reports `preset.audioBitrateStereoBps`, whatever the
source's channel count. `encoding.ts:54` picks correctly — it uses
`audioBitrateMonoBps` when `channelCount <= 1` — so the *encode* is right and
only the *estimate* is wrong. On a mono source at the smaller preset that is
128 kbps claimed against 96 kbps spent.

Small beside the 3.6x, and in the same direction, but it is in the same
function and should be fixed in the same pass: `outputShapeFor` needs the
channel count it is currently not given.

## Why it matters beyond tidiness

- It is shown **before** the user commits, so it is the number they decide on.
  Someone with a OneDrive limit in mind may pick "Smaller file" they did not
  need, and lose quality for nothing.
- It feeds VH-13, the published limits copy. Publishing figures derived from
  the same assumption would be publishing the same error.
- Being wrong in the *generous* direction is the safer half of the failure, but
  it still trains people to ignore the number.

## Two ways out

1. **Measure it.** The calibration probe already decodes three seconds of the
   real source. Encoding that sample and extrapolating would give a figure
   grounded in the actual content, at a cost the probe is already paying.
2. **Say what it is.** Present it as "no more than about 28 MB" rather than a
   point estimate. Cheap, honest, and much less likely to mislead — but it
   still overstates by a wide margin on compressible content.

Option 1 is the real fix and reuses machinery that exists. Option 2 is a
one-line honesty improvement worth doing meanwhile.

# Measured 2026-08-26, by an eight-agent design workflow

Everything below was measured against the real corpus with `h264_videotoolbox`
at the exact production targets — 46 whole-file re-encodes plus window encodes
for four candidate estimators. It is the expensive part; the design it produced
did NOT survive its own refutation and is recorded here as a starting point
rather than a plan.

## Two of this ticket's own premises are now false

**The 3.6x headline is stale.** VH-47 shipped after this ticket was written and
more than halved it. At HEAD, `outputShapeFor` gives AMCS3059
`videoBitrateBps: 610,031` on basis `blended-with-source` rather than the old
1.47 Mbps anchor, and `projectedOutputBytes` returns **12.7 MB** against the
7.5 MB the site produced. The live error is **1.70x**, verified independently by
hand. Half of this item was fixed by fixing something else.

**The over-estimate is NOT acting as a safety margin.** This ticket says it is.
At "Smaller file", today's projection falls BELOW the produced file on 4 of 23
jobs — CULT2011 0.991, Philosophy 0.997, CULT3033 0.999, AMCS2038 0.999. The
encoder overspends its target by up to 1.4% while the 1.02 container constant is
fully consumed by real overhead (measured 1.016 at 852x480, 1.005 at 1080p). So
the defect is "too big at Best quality and slightly too small at Smaller file",
and any fix that only shrinks the number makes the second half worse.

**The error is bimodal, so no single correction factor can work.** Ceiling
against actual: Best quality geo 1.474, median 1.037, max 4.784; Smaller file
geo 1.099, median 1.011, max 1.677. Wherever spec 6.2's cap binds or VH-47's
blend pulls the figure to the source, the ceiling is already exact.

## The estimator bake-off

Video term only, estimator/actual over 44-46 combinations:

| estimator | best: geo / med / min / max | smaller: geo / med / min / max |
| --- | --- | --- |
| today's ceiling | 1.559 / 1.125 / 0.998 / 4.705 | 1.095 / 1.001 / 0.986 / 1.723 |
| naive first 3 s | 0.453 / 0.684 / **0.049** / 2.213 | 0.742 / 0.817 / 0.269 / 1.550 |
| source-byte ratio | 1.073 / 1.037 / 0.391 / 2.826 | 1.759 / 1.193 / 0.608 / **15.645** |
| 3 x 9 s concatenated | 1.040 / 1.015 / 0.823 / 1.345 | 1.023 / 1.011 / 0.859 / 1.152 |

The first three seconds are unusable — 0.049 on Pelvis Sculpting, because a
3-second encode reproduces its requested bitrate to ~99.8% and therefore carries
almost no information about the file. The source-byte ratio is unusable: 15.6x
on a black lead-in. Long concatenated windows are the only estimator that works.

## The smallest concrete lever, verified by hand

`projectedOutputBytes(shape, report.durationSeconds)` (`job.worker.ts:300`)
multiplies by the SOURCE duration. The output is longer than that by the
closing — 4.00 s, or 5.00 s with a freeze — and `branding.ts:79` already owns
that rule as `closingAddedSeconds(mode)`, unused here. So the estimate omits
the tail outright: ~3% on a 130 s lecture, and it is part of WHY the four
"Smaller file" jobs measured below 1.0 land there.

It is a one-line fix and it is NOT free: correcting it raises the figure, and
the figure is multiplied by 2.5 into a hard storage block. It belongs with the
rest of this item rather than ahead of it.

## Why it was not implemented

ALL THREE adversarial refuters returned BLOCKING findings against the
recommendation, and they must be answered before any of it is built:

- **It raises `requiredStorageBytes` on 42 of 46 corpus combinations**, newly
  blocking jobs near the boundary — while the design's own text promises it
  does not. Storage is a hard block with no override.
- **The longer probe re-calibrates `videoFramesPerSecond` by 34-66%**, which
  moves `estimatedSeconds` across spec 7.3's 20-minute and 60-minute bands. The
  design states twice that it does not.
- **The wall budget cannot bound the probe**, because it is checked BETWEEN
  windows; and on 4K Best quality the early stop passes the minimum-sample floor
  so the panel shows a measured figure rather than the honest fallback.
- **Pre-flight already runs against a hard 180 s deadline with no keep-alive**,
  and this puts its single largest new cost inside that budget.
- **The estimator's error is driven by WHERE the expensive content sits**
  relative to the sample points, not by how many seconds are sampled — so the
  claimed 0.850 worst case is an artefact of these particular offsets.
- **The wall budget withdraws the fix from the files it exists to fix.** At
  8 s it stops early on exactly the large files that need estimating, on
  hardware only ~1.8x slower than the Mac it was measured on. The design costed
  itself on one machine. A two-window regime is then the modal outcome on a
  mid-speed laptop, has a measured 2x under-call, and was never scored at all.
- **`CONTAINER_OVERHEAD = 1.02` is measurably exceeded at the small-frame end**
  — the same end where the ceiling's margin is thinnest — so the two errors
  compound in the unsafe direction. Measured 1.0166 and 1.0174 on a 130 s
  852x480 file, so the constant is close but not a bound.

## What survived, and is worth keeping

- Two numbers, not one: a *shown* estimate and a separate *ceiling* for the
  storage gate, with the shown figure never reaching `PreflightInput`. Both
  refuters endorsed this half.
- `audioBitrateFor(preset, channelCount)` exported from `presets.ts` and used by
  BOTH `outputShapeFor` and `audioEncodingConfigFor`, so the mono/stereo rule
  has one decision site instead of two that disagree. Note a refuter found a
  THIRD site on the block path.
- Wording that states its own nature — "About 9 MB" when measured, "No more than
  about 190 MB" when not — at two significant figures, so it cannot read as a
  measurement. But `requiredStorageBytes` must round UP, not to two significant
  figures, or the block can ask for less space than the gate demands.
- The insufficient-storage message should stop saying the browser is at fault.
