UoN Video Helper

  • Lets you trim unwanted footage from the start and end.
  • Adds opening and closing graphics.
  • Evens out the audio level for better audience experience.
  • Saves in a reliable format.

    Videos come in many formats. Some play on one device but not another, or do not work with some apps. This tool saves your video as an MP4, the most interoperable video format. That means it plays on almost any phone, computer or website.

  • Makes the file the right size for your purpose.

Your video is processed on your device, it is never uploaded, and the original file does not change.

    Accessibility statement

    This statement covers UoN Video Helper, the page you are on.

    We want as many people as possible to be able to use this tool. You should be able to:

    • Use it with a keyboard alone.
    • Use it with a screen reader, which reads out each stage and result.
    • Zoom in to 200% without the text going off the screen.
    • Use your own colours, and your computer’s high contrast mode.
    • Turn off movement on the page in your computer’s settings.

    The words are plain, and the text has strong contrast with its background.

    How accessible this tool is

    We know some parts are not fully accessible:

    • It is built for Google Chrome on a computer. In other browsers, it may not work.
    • The Chinese and Malay pages were translated by a computer. We are asking colleagues who speak each language to check them, so some words may be wrong until they do.
    • Chinese text uses your computer’s own Chinese font, so it may look different from one computer to another.
    • The preview plays your own video as it is. Its captions, audio description and other alternatives, and whether it flashes, depend on your recording.
    • Some short forms are not spelled out. UoN stands for University of Nottingham. The others are file sizes, such as MB and GB, and video format names, such as H.264 and AAC.
    • The Choose File button belongs to your browser. On the Chinese and Malay pages, its words are in your browser’s language. To use it by voice, say the step’s name, “Choose a video”.

    Enforcement procedure

    Compliance status

    This tool is partially compliant with version 2.2 of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), at level AA, the middle of their three levels. This is because of the parts listed under “How accessible this tool is”, and because no one has yet tested it with a screen reader or other assistive technology. We aim for level AAA, the highest.

    How we tested this tool

    Each time the code changes, automated tests check the contrast of the text against its background, and the reading level of the English words.

    We have also checked it by hand in Chrome, in all three languages, at computer and phone widths, while building it.

    In October 2026, an automated check in Chrome went through the page’s main states in all three languages, at computer and phone widths, in light and dark colours, in Chrome’s imitation of high contrast mode, and with larger text. This was not a test by a person.

    What we plan to test next

    We have not yet run these tests. We need them before we can say the tool fully meets the guidelines:

    • Using the tool with a keyboard alone.
    • Using it with the NVDA screen reader in Chrome on Windows, and with VoiceOver on a Mac.
    • Zooming in to 400%, and using a screen 320 pixels wide.
    • Using Windows contrast themes.

    We are also thinking of:

    • A second automated tool, such as axe or Lighthouse.
    • A session with disabled staff who use the tool.
